A world model is an AI agent's internal simulator of reality. Instead of reacting to observations one at a time, an agent with a world model can *imagine* future states, plan ahead, and even train entirely inside its own mind.

The idea is older than deep learning — but the past few years have turned it into one of the most contested frontiers in AI research.

## The Core Idea

When you reach for a coffee cup, your brain doesn't compute from scratch. It predicts: *the cup is still there, my hand will close around it, it will feel warm*. That predictive loop is a world model — an internal compression of how the world works.

AI systems historically lacked this. A model trained on images learns correlations but has no notion of *what happens next* unless explicitly trained for it. World models change that by adding a forward-looking internal loop.

## How They Work

The canonical architecture has three parts:

- **Encoder** — compresses raw observations (pixels, sensor data) into a compact latent representation
- **Dynamics model** — predicts how that latent state evolves over time and in response to actions
- **Decoder / reward head** — reconstructs observations or predicts rewards from the latent

The agent can then *unroll* the dynamics model forward without ever touching the real environment — running thousands of simulated trajectories internally before acting.

```python
# Simplified DreamerV3-style latent imagination
z = encoder(observation)          # compress to latent
for _ in range(horizon):
    z = dynamics_model(z, action)  # imagine next state
    reward = reward_head(z)        # predict reward
    action = policy(z)             # plan in latent space
```

## Dreamer: Training in Dreams

The **Dreamer** line of work (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018 → DreamerV3, 2025) is the most influential practical implementation. Its key insight: train the policy *entirely inside the world model*, then deploy to the real environment.

DreamerV3 uses a **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM)** that maintains both a deterministic hidden state and a stochastic latent variable — capturing both the predictable and uncertain parts of the world.

The result: a single algorithm that masters Atari, continuous control, 3D navigation, and Minecraft diamond collection — without task-specific tuning.

## LeCun's Bet: JEPA

Yann LeCun has been arguing for years that **current LLMs are not world models** — they predict tokens, not the structure of reality. His alternative is **JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture)**: instead of predicting raw pixels or tokens, predict in *abstract embedding space*.

The key difference:

| Approach | Predicts | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Generative (LLMs, Sora) | Raw outputs (tokens, pixels) | Wastes capacity on irrelevant details |
| JEPA | Abstract representations | Learns structure, not surface |

In early 2026, LeCun left Meta to found **AMI** with $1B in funding — a direct bet that the path to general intelligence runs through world models, not scaled language models.

## Sora as a World Simulator?

OpenAI framed **Sora** (2024) as evidence that video generation models develop an implicit understanding of physical reality. Generating coherent video requires learning *something* about how objects move, collide, and interact.

But critics — including LeCun — argue this is surface-level simulation. A 2024 paper tested V-JEPA and Sora on physical consistency benchmarks and found both fail at multi-object interactions in ways a child wouldn't.

The debate remains open: is physical plausibility in generated video the same as *understanding* the world?

## Why It Matters

World models are the bridge between perception and planning. An agent that can simulate consequences before acting is more sample-efficient, safer to train, and potentially capable of the kind of flexible reasoning that current systems lack.

Whether the path runs through latent imagination (Dreamer), abstract prediction (JEPA), or emergent simulation in massive generative models (Sora) — every serious AI lab is now building toward the same goal: a system that doesn't just react to the world, but *models* it.
